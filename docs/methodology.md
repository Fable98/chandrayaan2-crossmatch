# Mathematical & Photogrammetric Methodology

This document details the mathematical formulations and photogrammetric principles implemented in the **Chandrayaan-2 Multi-Modal Cross-Sensor Image Registration Pipeline** for ISRO evaluators.

---

## 1. Illumination Invariance: 2D Log-Gabor Phase Congruency & CFOG

### 1.1 The Lunar Illumination Problem
Lunar orbital optical sensors (OHRC, TMC-2) capture the Moon under stark, unscattered solar illumination without an atmosphere. Variations in solar azimuth ($\psi_{\odot}$) and solar elevation ($\theta_{\odot}$) invert shadow directions across crater rims, shift apparent edge boundaries, and cause conventional intensity-gradient matchers (SIFT, SURF, NCC) to suffer severe correspondence failure or high outlier ratios.

### 1.2 2D Log-Gabor Filter Bank
Phase Congruency operates in the frequency domain, detecting features where the local Fourier harmonics are maximally in phase, independent of overall image contrast or amplitude.

A 2D Log-Gabor transfer function in polar frequency coordinates $(\rho, \theta)$ is defined as:
$$G_{s, o}(\rho, \theta) = \exp\left( -\frac{\left(\ln(\rho / \rho_{s})\right)^2}{2 \left(\ln(\sigma_{\rho} / \rho_{s})\right)^2} \right) \cdot \exp\left( -\frac{(\theta - \theta_{o})^2}{2 \sigma_{\theta}^2} \right)$$

where:
- $s \in \{1, \dots, N_s\}$ is the filter scale (radial frequency $\rho_s$).
- $o \in \{1, \dots, N_o\}$ is the filter orientation ($\theta_o = \frac{o \cdot \pi}{N_o}$).
- $\sigma_{\rho} / \rho_s$ controls the radial filter bandwidth (empirically set to $0.55$).
- $\sigma_{\theta}$ controls angular bandwidth ($\sigma_{\theta} \approx \frac{1.2 \pi}{N_o}$).

### 1.3 Local Energy Model & Phase Congruency
Convolving the grayscale image $I(x, y)$ with the even (cosine) and odd (sine) quadrature components of $G_{s, o}$ produces response components:
$$e_{s, o}(x, y) = I(x, y) * g_{s, o}^{\text{even}}, \quad o_{s, o}(x, y) = I(x, y) * g_{s, o}^{\text{odd}}$$

The local amplitude $A_{s, o}(x, y)$ and energy $E_o(x, y)$ at orientation $o$ are:
$$A_{s, o}(x, y) = \sqrt{e_{s, o}(x, y)^2 + o_{s, o}(x, y)^2}$$
$$E_o(x, y) = \sqrt{\left( \sum_s e_{s, o}(x, y) \right)^2 + \left( \sum_s o_{s, o}(x, y) \right)^2}$$

The 2D Phase Congruency $PC(x, y)$ is:
$$PC(x, y) = \frac{\sum_o \max\left(E_o(x, y) - T_o, 0\right)}{\sum_o \sum_s A_{s, o}(x, y) + \epsilon}$$
where $T_o$ is the orientation-dependent noise threshold estimated from Rayleigh distribution statistics, and $\epsilon$ prevents zero division.

### 1.4 Channel Features of Oriented Gradients (CFOG)
To capture multi-directional edge geometry, CFOG constructs a continuous 3D feature tensor $F(x, y, k)$ across $K$ orientation bins ($K = 8$, bin centers $\phi_k = \frac{k \pi}{K}$):
$$\nabla I = \left[ \frac{\partial PC}{\partial x}, \frac{\partial PC}{\partial y} \right]^T, \quad \theta(x, y) = \arctan\left(\frac{\partial PC / \partial y}{\partial PC / \partial x}\right), \quad M(x, y) = \|\nabla I\|_2$$
$$F(x, y, k) = M(x, y) \cdot \cos^2\left(\theta(x, y) - \phi_k\right) \quad \text{for } |\theta(x, y) - \phi_k| < \frac{\pi}{2}$$

Sub-pixel patch similarity is evaluated via normalized cross-correlation across all $K$ channels simultaneously:
$$\text{Sim}(P_1, P_2) = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^K \langle F_1^{(k)}, F_2^{(k)} \rangle}{\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^K \|F_1^{(k)}\|^2} \cdot \sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^K \|F_2^{(k)}\|^2}}$$

## 2. IIRS Hyperspectral Feature Engineering & Residual Decoupling

### 2.1 Principal Component Structural Synthesis
Chandrayaan-2 IIRS products consist of $B \le 256$ contiguous spectral channels spanning $0.8\text{--}5.0\,\mu\text{m}$. Directly averaging bands degrades contrast due to thermal emission and noise in deep IR channels.

To extract a coherent 2D structural base, we perform Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on the spectral covariance matrix:
$$\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times B}, \quad \bar{\mathbf{x}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbf{x}_i, \quad \mathbf{\Sigma} = \frac{1}{N} (\mathbf{X} - \bar{\mathbf{x}})^T (\mathbf{X} - \bar{\mathbf{x}})$$
$$\mathbf{\Sigma} \mathbf{v}_1 = \lambda_1 \mathbf{v}_1, \quad \mathbf{PC}_1 = (\mathbf{X} - \bar{\mathbf{x}}) \mathbf{v}_1$$

$\mathbf{PC}_1$ retains the dominant morphological surface topography across all non-redundant spectral bands.

### 2.2 Mineral Absorption Band Ratio Fusion
Crater rims and fresh ejecta expose mafic minerals (pyroxene, olivine) characterized by diagnostic absorption near $1000\,\text{nm}$. We compute a normalized mineral ratio index:
$$R_{\text{index}}(x, y) = \frac{R_{950}(x, y)}{\max\left(R_{750}(x, y), 10^{-4}\right)}$$

The final fused structural representation is generated by linear combination and Contrast-Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE):
$$S(x, y) = 0.75 \cdot \text{Norm}(\mathbf{PC}_1) + 0.25 \cdot \text{Norm}(R_{\text{index}})$$
$$I_{\text{IIRS\_struct}} = \text{CLAHE}(S(x, y))$$

### 2.3 Decoupling Spatial Misalignment from Spectral Angle Variance
To evaluate hyperspectral co-registration without confounding spatial error with spectral mixing, we independently report:
1. **Spatial Misalignment (Pixels)**:
   $$\text{RMSE}_{\text{spatial}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^M \left\| \mathbf{x}_{i, \text{dst}} - H \mathbf{x}_{i, \text{src}} \right\|_2^2}$$

2. **Spectral Angle Mapper (SAM) Deviation (Degrees)**:
   $$\alpha(x, y) = \arccos\left( \frac{\mathbf{s}(x, y)^T \bar{\mathbf{s}}}{\|\mathbf{s}(x, y)\|_2 \cdot \|\bar{\mathbf{s}}\|_2} \right) \cdot \frac{180^\circ}{\pi}$$
   where $\mathbf{s}(x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^B$ is the pixel reflectance vector and $\bar{\mathbf{s}}$ is the spatial mean spectrum.

## 3. Spatial Distribution: Grid Non-Maximum Suppression (Grid NMS)

### 3.1 Mitigation of Crater-Rim Match Clustering
Without spatial regulation, feature detectors cluster hundreds of keypoints along the highest-contrast feature (such as a single sharp crater rim), leaving the remainder of the swath unconstrained. This causes catastrophic projective lever-arm distortion across distant regions.

### 3.2 Grid NMS Algorithm
1. The working image space $\Omega = [0, W) \times [0, H)$ is partitioned into a uniform $G_x \times G_y$ grid of cells:
   $$C(g_x, g_y) = \left[ \frac{g_x W}{G_x}, \frac{(g_x + 1) W}{G_x} \right) \times \left[ \frac{g_y H}{G_y}, \frac{(g_y + 1) H}{G_y} \right)$$
2. All candidate matches $\mathcal{M} = \{m_i = (\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{y}_i, s_i)\}$ are assigned to their spatial cell:
   $$\text{Cell}(m_i) = \left( \left\lfloor \frac{x_i \cdot G_x}{W} \right\rfloor, \left\lfloor \frac{y_i \cdot G_y}{H} \right\rfloor \right)$$
3. Within each cell $C(g_x, g_y)$, candidate matches are sorted by confidence score $s_i$:
   $$\mathcal{M}_{g_x, g_y}^{\text{sorted}} = \text{SortDescending}\left(\{m \in \mathcal{M} \mid \text{Cell}(m) = (g_x, g_y)\}, \text{key}=s_i\right)$$
4. The top $K_{\text{max}}$ matches are retained per cell ($K_{\text{max}} = 4$):
   $$\mathcal{M}_{\text{filtered}} = \bigcup_{g_x=0}^{G_x-1} \bigcup_{g_y=0}^{G_y-1} \mathcal{M}_{g_x, g_y}^{\text{sorted}}\left[:K_{\text{max}}\right]$$

This enforces bounded spatial density and guarantees well-conditioned homography and piecewise estimation.

## 4. Topographic DEM-Aware Geometry & Piecewise Affine Warping

### 4.1 Lunar DEM Ray-Intersection
A planar projective homography $H \in \mathbb{P}^2$ assumes all surface points lie on a flat plane. For lunar topography observed with off-nadir emission angle $\theta_{\text{em}}$ and azimuth $\psi_{\text{az}}$, relief displacement $\vec{\delta}$ is proportional to the elevation delta $\Delta z$:
$$\vec{\delta}_{\text{relief}} = \left[ \Delta x_{\text{px}}, \Delta y_{\text{px}} \right]^T = \frac{\Delta z \cdot \tan(\theta_{\text{em}})}{\text{GSD}} \begin{bmatrix} \cos(\psi_{\text{az}}) \\ \sin(\psi_{\text{az}}) \end{bmatrix}$$

For rigorous ray-intersection, we march rays through the DEM height field iteratively:
$$\mathbf{p}^{(k+1)} = \mathbf{p}^{(0)} + \frac{\left(z(\mathbf{p}^{(k)}) - z_{\text{datum}}\right) \cdot \tan(\theta_{\text{em}})}{\text{GSD}} \begin{bmatrix} \cos(\psi_{\text{az}}) \\ \sin(\psi_{\text{az}}) \end{bmatrix}$$
until $\|\mathbf{p}^{(k+1)} - \mathbf{p}^{(k)}\|_2 \cdot \text{GSD} < \epsilon_{\text{tol}}$ ($0.5\,\text{m}$).

### 4.2 DEM-Aware RANSAC Model Fitting
Before sampling random minimal 4-point subsets, source keypoints are corrected by their relief displacement vector:
$$\mathbf{p}_{i, \text{corrected}} = \mathbf{p}_{i, \text{src}} + \vec{\delta}_{\text{relief}}(\mathbf{p}_{i, \text{src}})$$
Homography estimation $H_{\text{relief}}$ is then performed in parallax-compensated coordinates, eliminating relief outliers.

### 4.3 Overlapping Piecewise Affine Warping with Hann Blending
Rather than warping the entire scene with a single global planar transform, the output raster is reconstructed across overlapping tiles of size $T \times T$ with overlap fraction $\beta = 0.25$.
For each tile $t$, a local affine transform $A_t \in \text{Aff}(2)$ is fitted to local inliers. Tiles are composited using a 2D separable Hann (cosine) blending window:
$$w_x(u) = 0.5 \left( 1 - \cos\left( \frac{2\pi u}{T} \right) \right), \quad w_y(v) = 0.5 \left( 1 - \cos\left( \frac{2\pi v}{T} \right) \right)$$
$$W_t(u, v) = w_x(u) \cdot w_y(v)$$
$$I_{\text{out}}(x, y) = \frac{\sum_t W_t(x, y) \cdot \left( A_t^{-1} \circ I_{\text{src}} \right)(x, y)}{\sum_t W_t(x, y)}$$

This completely eliminates seam artifacts and accommodates continuous crater wall elevation variations.

## 5. Metric Error Formulation: Absolute 3D Selenodetic RMSE

### 5.1 Planar Pixel vs. Selenodetic Metric Error
Evaluation solely in pixel units fails across multi-resolution instruments (e.g., $1\,\text{px}$ in OHRC is $0.25\,\text{m}$, while $1\,\text{px}$ in TMC-2 is $5.0\,\text{m}$).

### 5.2 3D Metric RMSE Formulation
Given $N$ verified correspondences with 2D coordinates $(\mathbf{x}_{i, \text{src}}, \mathbf{y}_{i, \text{dst}})$ and Ground Sample Distance $\text{GSD}$:
$$\Delta x_i = \left( \hat{x}_{i, \text{dst}} - x_{i, \text{dst}} \right) \cdot \text{GSD}_x$$
$$\Delta y_i = \left( \hat{y}_{i, \text{dst}} - y_{i, \text{dst}} \right) \cdot \text{GSD}_y$$
where $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{i, \text{dst}} = H \mathbf{x}_{i, \text{src}}$.

When DEM elevation data $Z(\mathbf{x})$ is available:
$$\Delta z_i = Z(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{i, \text{dst}}) - Z(\mathbf{x}_{i, \text{src}})$$
$$\text{RMSE}_{\text{metric}} = \sqrt{ \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \left( \Delta x_i^2 + \Delta y_i^2 + \Delta z_i^2 \right) } \quad [\text{meters}]$$

When DEM is unavailable (planar datum approximation):
$$\text{RMSE}_{\text{metric, 2D}} = \sqrt{ \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \left( \Delta x_i^2 + \Delta y_i^2 \right) } = \text{RMSE}_{\text{pixel}} \cdot \text{GSD} \quad [\text{meters}]$$

This metric provides the official physical accuracy benchmark for orbital cross-referencing.
